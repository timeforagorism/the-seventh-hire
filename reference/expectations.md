# What the team expects, and when

The team is five weeks from a hard date. A first day is a lot to take in, and a new person joining now worries about two things: being useless, and being in the way. This file is what the team actually expects. There are checkpoints at three months, six months and a year, and nothing is being measured this week.

## Day one

- Everything opens, the new hire has met the team, and modules 1 and 2 are watched. The detail is in `first-day.md`.
- Nobody expects any work product on day one.

## Week one

- All ten Foundations modules watched by Friday, two a day.
- During the week, read the billing design summary and the two most recent client meeting notes in the repository. Once through is enough.
- At standup every morning, listening. Speaking starts in week two. What to say is in Nadia's notes, `questions-answered.md`.
- Thursday afternoon: sit with Jane while she fixes one defect, start to finish. The new hire watches.
- Friday: first timesheet in by 3:00, and thirty minutes with Jane at 3:30 to talk about how the week went.
- Nobody expects a fix, a design opinion or a finished task in week one.

## The checkpoints

Three sit-downs with Jane and Dana, at three months, six months and a year. They are something to work toward, and nobody is expected to be there early. Each one is a conversation about where the new hire is. Nobody keeps a score.

CIS takes time to come to people. Everyone on the team started where the new hire is starting. Jane says her own first month was mostly nodding.

### Three months: comfortable

- The new hire feels fairly comfortable working in CIS and its tools.
- The work until then is small pieces in different areas of the system: a notices fix one week, a solar fix the next. The first one comes in week two, from the first trial conversion's defect list.
- They are small and spread out on purpose, so the new hire sees a little of a lot of the system. It says nothing about what the team thinks they can handle. Dana picks them that way for everyone, including people with ten years behind them.
- Jane reviews every change before it goes anywhere.
- The new hire is off the roster for the trial conversion weekend. They come in on the Monday after and help Joanne check results.

### Six months: starting to click

- Things have started to connect. A change in one area suggests what it will do to another.
- The work gets larger and less defined. The new hire is given a problem that does not come with its answer and has to work out how they would solve it.
- They work more on their own and still bring every proposal back for review.

### One year: finding your own answers

- The new hire finds a lot more answers on their own. They can debug CIS and work out how a piece of it behaves without someone sitting beside them.
- Ostrander sells one product to every customer. It is large, and nobody learns it in a month. The team's own estimate is a year.
- The checkpoints follow the person and carry on after this project. Go-live comes before the one-year mark.
- Until then, asking is the job. The team would sooner answer a question than find out a week later that it was never asked.

## How work is handed over

This is the team's usual way, from the first small piece to the large ones.

1. Jane gives the new hire a problem and explains it.
2. The new hire goes away and looks into it.
3. They come back with a proposed solution, before building anything.
4. Jane reviews the proposal with them. It is a conversation, and the new hire's ideas get bounced around.
5. Then the work gets done.

Nobody is expected to come back with the right answer. They are expected to come back with a proposal and the reasons for it.

## Questions

- Every question goes to Jane, on one list, at 4:15. The rule, the list and the exceptions are in `who-owns-what.md`.

## The other new starters

- Two other people started at Ostrander on the same Monday, on other teams on this site: Owen Brandt and Leila Haddad. They are watching the same Foundations modules the same week.
- The team calls the three of them war buddies. Whatever one of them is stuck on, the other two are probably about to hit.
- Sharing what was learned with them is encouraged, and so is asking them first when a module does not make sense. They are the one group a new hire can go to any time without costing the project anything.

## Writing answers down

- The team is building its own knowledge base as it goes. It lives in the project repository.
- When a new hire gets an answer that was not written anywhere, they add it to `new-hire/questions-answered.md` the same day: the question, the answer, who gave it, the date.
- This is an expectation, the same as the timesheet. It is the one thing a new hire can give the team in week one.
- Nadia Farouk started that file eight months ago with every question from her first week. A copy of her part is in this folder as `questions-answered.md`. Whatever the new hire is about to ask, there is a fair chance she asked it.
